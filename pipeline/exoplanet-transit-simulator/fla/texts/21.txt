This simulator shows what happens when an extrasolar planet passes in 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
front of its star. Such eclipses (called transits) allow scientists to detect 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
planets they would not see otherwise.
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
The upper left panel shows the star and planet as they would be seen 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
from earth if we had an extremely powerful telescope. In fact, this 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
privileged view is impossible with any existing telescope; astronomers 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
must infer the transit from the dip in the lightcurve as the planet moves in 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
front of the star. (A lightcurve is a plot of brightness over time.)
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
The lightcurve is shown in the upper right panel. The apparent brightness 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
of the star is 'normalized', that is, the brightness is reported as a fraction 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
of the full brightness (when the star is not eclipsed). You have the option 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
of showing simulated noisy measurements and hiding the theoretical 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
curve – this gives a better idea of what it is like working with real data.
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
The planet, star, and system properties can be set in the lower panels. 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
These parameters can also be set by selecting one of the presets from the 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
drop down menu in the Presets panel.
