This simulator models the motion of an extrasolar planet and its star 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
around their common center of mass. Although the star does not move 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
very much scientists can use the doppler shift of the star's absorption 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
lines to determine its speed with great accuracy. This allows them to infer 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
the existence and properties of a planet they can not see directly.

The panel in the upper left shows the system and its orientation in space 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
with respect to the earth. In the Visualization Controls panel beneath the 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
visualization there is an option to show several different perspectives at 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
the same time. The view from earth is the most important since it is the 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
star's velocity along the line of sight that we measure with the radial 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
velocity technique. Note that the sizes of the planet and star discs are not 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
to scale – this is done for clarity and does not affect the radial velocity.
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
The radial velocity plot is shown in the upper right panel. This plot shows 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
how fast the star is moving along the earth's line of sight.

It is important 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
to realize that the velocity is that of the star even though the star may 
--- RECORDSEPARATOR ---
appear to move very little, or not at all, in the visualization panel.

 This
