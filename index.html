KL-UNL Simulations

Antipodes Explorer
This tool demonstrates antipodal points, which are points on opposite sides of the earth from each other.

Azimuth Altitude Demonstrator
This demonstrator illustrates the horizon coordinate system, where altitude and azimuth define an object's position in the sky.

Basketball Phases Simulator
This simulator shows an illuminated basketball that can be viewed from multiple directions, providing an analogy to moon phases.

Big Dipper 3D
This simulator demonstrates how the stars of the big dipper, which are at various distances from earth, project onto the celestial sphere to give the familiar asterism.

Big Dipper Clock
This clock shows how stars rotate around the North Star over time (both daily and seasonal motions are shown).

Blackbody Curves of Melting
The apparatus shown is a common demonstration piece in introductory science classes. A nail is placed under tension and a large electric current is allowed to flow through it. The nail's temperature rises until the iron melts and the tension pulls the nail apart, breaking the circuit. The way in which color and brightness change with temperature illustrates Wien's and Stefan-Boltzmann's Laws.

Blackbody Explorer
This explorer demonstrates how the blackbody spectrum – that is, the energy emitted by a thermal source – varies with temperature. Stars, to a reasonable first approximation, can be considered as blackbodies.

Blink Comparator Simulator
This simulator cycles through multiple images of the same field, revealing variable stars. The variable stars identified in this simulator can then be analyzed with the variable star Photometry Analyzer.

Celestial and Horizon Systems Comparison
This demonstrator shows how the celestial sphere and horizon diagram are related.

Celestial Equatorial RA Dec Demonstrator
This demonstrator illustrates the celestial-equatorial (RA/Dec) coordinate system, where declination and right ascension define an object's position on the celestial sphere.

Center of Mass Simulator
This simulator shows how the center of mass of two objects changes as their masses change.

Circumstellar Habitable Zone Simulator
This simulator demonstrates the location and evolution of the stellar habitable zone, the region around a star where surface water may exist on an earth like planet.

Coordinate Systems Comparison
This simulator is designed to show how the rotation of the earth leads to the apparent rotation of the sky and associated diurnal phenomena.

Daylight Hour Explorer
This graph shows the hours of daylight received during the year for an observer at a given latitude. This is an important factor contributing to the seasons.

Daylight Simulator
This simulator illustrates daylight and nighttime regions on a flat map of earth. Daily and yearly motions of the sunlight pattern can be shown.

Declination Ranges Simulator
This simulator shows how an observer's latitude determines the circumpolar, rise and set, and never rise regions in the sky.

Earth Orbit Plot
This plot shows the orbital period as a function of orbital distance for satellites of earth.

Eccentricity Demonstrator
This demonstrator illustrates the parameters that define the eccentricity of an ellipse.

Eclipse Shadow Simulator
This simulator provides draggable earth and moon discs with shadows, which can be used to demonstrate how the umbral (complete) and penumbral (partial) shadows give rise to different types of eclipses.

Eclipse Table
This table lists solar and lunar eclipses, showing the banding that represents the eclipse seasons that occur about twice a year.

Eclipsing Binary Simulator
This simulator models the motions of two stars in orbit around each other. When such a system is aligned properly the stars will eclipse one another, causing a change in brightness (as seen from earth) that allows astronomers to determine the properties of the stars.

Ecliptic Simulator
This simulator shows the geometry of the earth as it goes around the sun, demonstrating why seasons occur.

Ecliptic Zodiac Simulator
This simulator shows the sun's position in the sky relative to the background stars (the zodiac constellations) over the course of a year.

Epicycles Demo
This demonstrator shows that the heliocentric and geocentric models are equivalent for predictive purposes when limited to circular orbits. One can watch the same system in motion with different bodies held in fixed position.

Exoplanet Transit Simulator
This simulator shows what happens when an extrasolar planet passes in front of its star. Such eclipses (called transits) allow scientists to detect planets they would not see otherwise.

Extrasolar Planet Radial Velocity Demonstrator
This demonstrator shows how the spectrum of a star is shifted as it and its planet orbit their common center of mass.

Full Moon Declination Demonstrator
This demonstrator shows the declination range of the full moon over the course of a year, and the corresponding changes in altitude for a northern hemisphere observer.

Galactic Redshift Simulator
This simulator demonstrates the redshift of a galaxy due to the expansion of the universe, and the effect this shift has on the galaxy's brightness as observed through various filters.

Gas Retention Plot
This interactive plot shows how molecular mass, temperature and escape speed determine whether a gas will remain gravitationally bound to a planet.

Gas Retention Simulator
This simulator demonstrates how gases of different molecular masses behave when maintained at thermodynamic equilibrium in a chamber. The chamber can be set to allow particles that exceed a certain speed to escape, providing an analogy for the bleeding off of a planet’s atmosphere into space.

Gravity Algebra
This demonstrator shows how the force of gravity would be different if the values used in Newton's law of universal gravitation formula were changed.

Heliacal Rising Simulator
This simulator demonstrates heliacal rising, which occurs on the day when a star first becomes visible in the morning twilight.

HR Diagram Cluster Fitting Explorer
This explorer allows one to determine the distance to a cluster by fitting the cluster's stars to the main sequence in an HR diagram.

Hydrogen Atom Simulator
This simulator models the interaction of a hydrogen atom with light, demonstrating the quantum nature of absorption and emission.

Influence of Planets on the Sun
This explorer shows the movement of the sun due to the gravitational pull of the planets.

Kepler's Third-Law
This calculator lets one compute the period of a planet (in earth years) from its semimajor axis (in astronomical units), and vice versa.

Longitude Latitude-Demo
This demonstrator illustrates latitude and longitude with an interactive globe, providing an analogy to the celestial and horizon coordinate systems.

Lookback Time Simulator
This simulator shows how the finite speed of light and the great distances to most astronomical objects cause us to observe things as they were in the past.

Lunar Phase Quizzer
This quizzer shows the standard orbital view of the moon, but with the option to hide the moon's phase, the moon's position, or the sun's direction.

Lunar Phases Simulator
This simulator demonstrates the correspondence between the moon's position in its orbit, its phase, and its position in an observer's sky at different times of day.

Lunar Phases Vocabulary
This demonstrator shows the appearance of the moon at each of the named phases.

Meridional Altitude Simulator
This diagram shows the geometry for calculating the meridional altitude of objects.

Milky Way Habitability Explorer
This explorer shows how two factors important to life – metallicity and extinction risk – vary throughout the Milky Way Galaxy.

Milky Way Rotational Velocity Explorer
This explorer shows a plot of the rotational velocity of stars at varying distances from the center of the milky way. A draggable cursor allows determining the contained mass implied by the curve.

Moon Inclination Demo
This demonstrator shows the correspondence between the moon's position in its orbit, its phase, and its position in an observer's sky at different times of day.

Moon Phases and Horizon Diagram
This demonstrator is designed for teaching subjects like rising and setting times, time of day, and moon phases. The emphasis is on how these topics relate to the positions of the sun and moon in an observer's horizon diagram.

Moon Phases with Bisectors
This simulator demonstrates a method for determining moon phases using planes the bisect the earth and moon.

Newton's Law of Gravity Calculator
This calculator lets one compute the force of gravity acting on a variety of masses over a range of distances.

Obliquity Simulator
This simulator shows how obliquity (tilt) is defined.

The earth's rotation axis (the cream line through the globe) is tilted away from the vertical — the line perpendicular to the plane of the ecliptic — by the obliquity angle.

Parallax Calculator
This calculator diagrams the geometry and shows the math involved in determining a star's distance via parallax.

Parallax Explorer
This explorer demonstrates the concept of parallax and how it can be used to determine distance using an analogy of a boat on a lake.

Phase Positions Demonstrator
This demonstrator shows how planet and moon phases depend on the viewing geometry.

Phases of Venus
This demonstrator shows what Venus would look like through a telescope if Ptolemy's model was correct.

Photometry Simulator
This simulator demonstrates aperture photometry by allowing one to drag aperture discs over a star field.

Planet Formation Temperatures Plot
This explorer shows planet formation temperature as a function of distance from the Sun. Also indicates the state (gas or solid) of several substances at the given distance and temperature.

Planetary Configurations Simulator
This simulator models the movement of planets around the sun in a simplified Copernican model of the solar system. Special emphasis is placed on the elongation angle and associated events.

Planetary Orbit Simulator
This simulator models the motion of a hypothetical planet that orbits the sun according to Kepler's laws of motion.

Ptolemaic Orbits of Mars
This demonstrator shows Ptolemy's model for the orbit of mars.

Ptolemaic Phases of Venus
This demonstrator shows what Venus would look like through a telescope if Ptolemy's model was correct.

Ptolemaic System Simulator
This simulator demonstrates how Ptolemy’s geocentric model accounts for the movements of the planets. In this model the sun, moon, and planets orbit the stationary earth. A planet’s path is described by a circle on circle construction: a planet moves on a circle called the epicycle which in turn moves on a larger circle called the deferent.

Pulsar Period Simulator
This simulator demonstrates how the movement of a pulsar and planet around their common center of mass affects the timing of pulse arrivals.

Radial Velocity Simulator
This simulator models the motion of an extrasolar planet and its star around their common center of mass. Although the star does not move very much scientists can use the doppler shift of the star’s absorption lines to determine its speed with great accuracy. This allows them to infer the existence and properties of a planet they can not see directly.

Registration Simulator
This simulator allows one to adjust the relative positions of three images so that their contents are aligned.

Retrograde Motion
This demonstrator shows the retrograde motion of mars with an annotated animation.

Sidereal and Solar Time Simulation
This simulator demonstrates the difference between sidereal and solar time.

Sidereal Time and Hour Angle Demo
This demonstrator shows how sidereal time and the hour angle of a star are related.

Small Angle Demo
This demonstrator shows how small angles can be approximated.

Solar System Properties Explorer
This explorer allows one to explore a set of histograms for characteristics like number of satellites, mass, orbital period, etc. for the terrestrial and jovian planets, plus Pluto.

Spectroscopic Parallax Simulator
This simulator demonstrates how the technique of spectroscopic parallax works. Spectral type and luminosity class determine the observed spectrum of a star, from which the star's luminosity can be estimated.

Spectrum Explorer
This explorer allows one to generate a variety of simulated spectra, depending on factors such as the type of source, luminosity class, spectral type, and individually selected elements.

Sun Motion Demo
This simulator models the motions of the sun in the sky using a horizon diagram, demonstrating daily and seasonal changes in the sun's position.

Sun Motions Overview
This overview shows the paths of the sun on the celestial sphere.

Sun Path Demo
This demonstrator shows how the declination of the sun varies over the course of a year using a horizon diagram.

Sun Position on Horizon
This simulator shows how the direction of the sun at sunrise or sunset changes over the course of the year.

Sun Rays Simulator
This simulator shows how the sun's most direct rays hit different parts of the earth as the seasons change.

Supernova Light Curve Fitting Explorer
This explorer allows one to determine the distance to a supernova by fitting observations to a theoretical Type Ia light curve.

Variable Star Photometry Analyzer
This analyzer demonstrates variable star analysis through period determination and light curve folding.

Synodic Lag
This demonstrator shows the difference between a sidereal and synodic (solar) day, which arises from the earth's revolution around the sun.

Synodic Periodic Calculator
This calculator lets one compute the sidereal period of the planet (P) from the synodic period (S), and vice versa.

Three View Spectral Simulator
This demonstrator shows how different spectra can arise from a light bulb (a thermal source) and a cold, thin gas cloud. The spectrometer shows emission, absorption, or continuous spectra based on where the draggable telescope is pointed.

Three Views Simulator
This simulator shows how the phase of the moon depends on the viewing geometry by allowing the moon to be viewed from the earth, the sun, and an arbitrary point in space.

Tidal Bulge Simulation
This simulator shows how the sun, moon, and earth's rotation combine to create tides.

Union Seasons Demonstrator
This file demonstrates seasonal changes through a sequence of images taken over the course of a year. Each of the images was taken when the sun reached its highest point in the sky (the meridional transit). The camera was pointed east, so the direction of the sun is up and to the right.
